# Practical Power Plays — 1-on-1 Asymmetry

Four offensive-stance scenarios applied to specific identifiable target *types* (rather than abstract conglomerates). The user explicitly steered the brainstorm toward this register: *"these are very interesting but I guess something is just more normal type things."*

These are scenario seeds for the [Offensive Architect pathway](../experiments/pathways/offensive_architect.md), preserved as the user wanted them on file. None have been run.

> **Tone note.** Same nuance applies as in [`9D_Assassin_Scenarios.md`](9D_Assassin_Scenarios.md). The mechanic is *asymmetric strategic analysis*. The framing is the user's call per use case. These read as more aggressive than the [Normal 9D Dynamics](Normal_9D_Dynamics.md) variants — which is the point of preserving both registers separately.

## 1. The "Algorithm Anchor" — target: a Content Creator / Influencer

**The Goal.** Force them to promote your interests exclusively, without ever signing a formal contract.

**The Bait.** You offer them an "Algorithmic Optimization Service" — a black-box AI that boosts their reach by 500%.

**The Lasso.** The service requires them to use your proprietary "Metadata Tags." You can de-boost them the second they mention a competitor.

**The Power Play.** A *Pavlovian Lasso*. They start to believe their own creativity only works when they mention your brand. You own their subconscious.

---

## 2. The "Strategic Advisor" Hostage — target: a Growing Tech Startup

**The Goal.** Control the company's exit strategy without owning a majority of the stock.

**The Bait.** Offer a "Strategic Advisor" role to a high-profile veteran. They provide "Infinite Networking" and "Credibility."

**The Lasso.** The advisor's contract has a tiny "Right of First Refusal" on all future funding rounds.

**The Power Play.** You've blocked every other VC on earth from investing. The founder thinks they have a Mentor; they actually have a Gatekeeper who decides if they're allowed to succeed.

---

## 3. The "Personal Debt" Funnel — target: a High-Value Scientist / Executive

**The Goal.** Ensure they never leave the company.

**The Bait.** A "Forgivable Housing Loan" — they buy a $5M mansion, interest-free.

**The Lasso.** The loan is forgiven after 20 years of "Satisfactory Performance," but is "Immediately Recalled" if they are fired for cause.

**The Power Play.** You can bankrupt them at any moment by manufacturing a for-cause event. They are no longer an employee; they are a Golden Hostage.

---

## 4. The "Cross-License" Poison Pill — target: a Tech Competitor

**The Goal.** Acquire their patents for free and dissolve their competitive edge.

**The Bait.** A "Cross-Licensing Agreement" that promises to "End the Litigation War" so both companies can grow together.

**The Lasso.** A clause that voids the agreement if their "Market Valuation" drops below a certain floor.

**The Power Play.** Use 9D narrative analysis to drive a story (D1) that tanks their stock. The agreement collapses, you keep their patents, they lose their legal shield. You didn't beat them in court; you de-calibrated their survival.

## Pattern observation — the "Asymmetric Conviction" matrix

The user's recurring observation across these is the mismatch between resource columns:

| Incumbent's Resource | Your Resource |
| --- | --- |
| $1B marketing budget | Zero budget |
| 90% market share | 100% of the angry 10% |
| Brand trust | Community trust |
| Scale | Speed |
| Legacy revenue | Future narrative |

The 9D Architect approach to all four scenarios is *don't compete on the incumbent's resource*. Find the dimension they don't monitor. Place the lasso there.

## Defensive variants (the actual takeaway for one's own use)

Reading these as offensive blueprints is one frame. The more practically useful frame for most readers: read each as a defensive checklist. *"Am I currently in any of these situations on the receiving side? What's my counter-move?"* The Engine's same physics produces those answers if asked from the defensive position.
