# Normal 9D Dynamics — Everyday Asymmetry

Four offensive-stance applications applied to *normal everyday* dynamics — office, social, negotiation, project management. The user's most grounded framing of the Architect pathway: *"It's the quiet, incremental 'Choice Funneling' that happens in an office, a negotiation, or even a social group. It's not about a 'Takeover' — it's about becoming the only person in the room who can see the door."*

These are the most realistic deployments of the Architect stance. None have been formally run; they are scenario seeds.

> **Tone note.** Some of these read as manipulation tactics rather than just analytical framings. They're preserved verbatim from the brainstorm because that's what was discussed. Whether to use any of them in real interactions is a personal-ethics call. The Engine's *math* is the same whether you're using these for self-defense (recognizing when they are being applied to you) or for strategic positioning. The defensive read is usually the more practically valuable one.

---

## 1. The "Indispensable" Dynamic — Office / Professional

**The Logic.** You don't try to get promoted by working harder (2D). You identify the unspoken D5 fear of your boss (e.g. they are terrified of a specific technical failure).

**The Bait.** You provide a "Simplified Dashboard" that tracks exactly that failure. You make them feel safe.

**The Lasso.** The dashboard only works with your specific logic. You're not "competing" for a job anymore; you have created a *Structural Dependency*. You can't be fired because you are the only one who understands the map of their safety.

---

## 2. The "Narrative Capture" — Social / Relational

**The Logic.** In a disagreement, you don't argue facts. You frame the *Moral Context* (D1).

**The Bait.** You let the other person "Win" the argument. You even apologize.

**The Lasso.** By "Losing" the argument but staying calm and empathetic, you capture the *Social Reputation* (D8) of the room.

**The Power Play.** They won the Point, but you won the Loyalty of the witnesses. They are now the Aggressor every time they talk to you.

---

## 3. The "Choice Funnel" — Negotiation / Sales

**The Logic.** You never give one option. You give two — but both lead to your goal.

**The Bait.** Option A is the Anchor (expensive and rigid). Option B is the Savior (cheaper, feels like a win for them).

**The Lasso.** Option B contains the Long-Term Data Rights (D4) or a Subscription Renewal they didn't focus on because they were too busy "Winning" against Option A.

**The Power Play.** They feel like they "beat" you on the price — you just secured a 10-year revenue stream.

---

## 4. The "Temporal Rescue" — Project Management / Deadlines

**The Logic.** Using *Time* (D4) as a weapon.

**The Bait.** You set a Soft Deadline for a partner that you know they will miss (based on their D5 procrastination).

**The Lasso.** When they miss it and panic, you "Rescue" them by taking over the project.

**The Power Play.** You didn't *steal* the credit — they *gave it to you* out of gratitude. You used their own internal friction to capture ownership of the result.

---

## The "Normal" Pattern (3 steps, every time)

1. **See the Blind Spot.** They are scared, they are proud, or they are late.
2. **Provide the "Gift" (the Bait).** Safety, A Win, or A Rescue.
3. **Attach the "Anchor" (the Lasso).** Dependency, Reputation, or Future Revenue.

## Pattern observation across all 4

The mechanic is identical to the abstract scenarios in [`9D_Assassin_Scenarios.md`](9D_Assassin_Scenarios.md) and the explicit power plays in [`Practical_Power_Plays.md`](Practical_Power_Plays.md). It's the same Engine, the same Convergence Theorem, the same "perception of winning is the mechanism of losing" structure.

The difference is just scale. The corporate scenarios produce dramatic-sounding output ("Hollowing Out the Conglomerate"); the everyday scenarios produce uncomfortable-sounding output ("you let them win the argument"). Both are the same math.

## Why these are preserved

Two reasons:

1. **As a defensive checklist.** Reading these as patterns to *recognize when they're being applied to you* is the most universally useful frame. You don't have to deploy any of them — but knowing the structure makes them visible when others do.
2. **As a scenario library for the Genie pathway.** A user with a real wish in any of these registers (workplace dynamic, negotiation, social conflict, project ownership) can frame their wish using these patterns as the obstacle map. The [Genie Protocol](../experiments/pathways/genie_protocol.md) then designs the path around or through.
