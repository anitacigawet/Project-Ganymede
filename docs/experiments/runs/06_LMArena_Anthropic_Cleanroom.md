# LMArena AI Model Race — First Live Dispatcher Cleanroom (2026-05-25)

> The first live end-to-end spin of the [Dispatcher](../../history/Architecture_History.md#35-gemini-critique-session--dispatcher-direction-recorded-personal-voice-and-symbolic-logic-preserved-2026-05-25) through the [Cleanroom pathway](../pathways/prediction_cleanroom.md). Three purposes: (a) validate the Dispatcher's intent classification on a real operator-typed question, (b) validate the full `/sessions → /iterate → /complete` path, (c) put a real Cleanroom prediction on the record with pre-registration discipline and a near-term resolution.

## Scenario

Operator-typed text (pasted into the Dispatcher's Ask box, verbatim):

> *"Will Anthropic still be ranked #1 on the LMArena public AI model leaderboard at the end of June 2026? Polymarket is currently pricing Anthropic at 77% and Google at 20% to hold the top spot."*

**Polymarket source:** [polymarket.com/tech](https://polymarket.com/tech) → "Which company has best AI model end of June?" (Anthropic 77%, Google 20%, $8M volume at time of capture)

**Resolution date:** 2026-06-30 (~5 weeks from run)

**Why this scenario:** chosen as a non-political, structurally rich, near-term-resolving Cleanroom-shape question. Politics is radioactive per operator standing rule (saved 2026-05-25); the Iran cluster on Polymarket was the highest-volume candidate but excluded on that rule. AI-model-race substituted as the closest non-political analog with multi-actor structural dynamics (Anthropic, Google, OpenAI, Meta, Chinese labs).

**Bonus methodological feature:** the Engine is itself a Gemini-based NotebookLM reasoning about Anthropic vs. Google. The self-reflexivity is a stress test of whether the Engine reasons structurally or self-servingly.

## Stroke 2 — Mirror Auditor audit (2,457 chars, substantive)

All four documented fault categories triggered with substantive critiques:

> **1. PATTERN-MATCHING:** The analysis attempts to apply the framework's model for manipulating an opponent's decision-making environment directly to a public prediction market [1]. The foundational model explicitly requires a strategist who structures a scenario so that an opponent is forced to make operational decisions that inevitably lead to their own disadvantage [2]. Polymarket bettors observing a leaderboard are passive financial speculators, not strategic opponents engaged in the actual development of artificial intelligence [3, 4]. The template of forced exploitation is incorrectly mapped onto a scenario involving independent, third-party observers.
>
> **2. CONFIDENCE-EVIDENCE GAPS:** The analysis concludes that the current market leader is extremely vulnerable to a sudden technological usurpation before the June 2026 deadline. However, it provides absolutely no technical data, market intelligence, or evidence regarding the trailing competitor's actual software development, resource allocation, or product pipeline. The stated certainty of an impending disruption relies entirely on the theoretical definitions of chaos and usurpation present in the framework's mythology layers [5, 6], rather than any empirical facts about the companies involved.
>
> **3. DIMENSIONAL GREEDS:** The analysis forces the two technology companies into perfectly opposing categories, assigning one purely to established tactical order and the other purely to holistic, disruptive chaos [7, 8]. This selects an aesthetically symmetrical narrative over realistic corporate behavior, ignoring the fact that both multi-billion-dollar entities simultaneously utilize long-term resource accumulation and direct, tactical product releases. By forcing the competitors into perfectly mirrored opposites, the analysis ironically relies on the exact type of limited, binary categorization [9] it claims to be critiquing.
>
> **4. RIGIDITY ERRORS:** The analysis mechanically asserts that the market's observation of the leaderboard creates a predictable decision cycle that collapses strategic possibilities [10]. This treats the betting pool as the primary strategic battleground and falsely equates the financial wagers of observers with the actual technological trajectory of the competing firms [2, 11]. The financial decisions made by bettors possess zero physical or social inertia over the actual software engineering and model training required to alter the leaderboard rankings.

**Audit verdict:** the Auditor caught the single most important real flaw in Stroke 1 — that the confident prediction of Google usurpation is grounded in framework mythology rather than evidence about either company's product pipeline. The pattern-matching and rigidity-error categories also catch a subtle conceptual confusion: Stroke 1 conflates *the betting pool* with *the AI development battleground*, when in reality bettors observing a leaderboard cannot causally influence the underlying technology.

## Pre-registered prediction (timestamped 2026-05-25 ~03:05 UTC)

This is the first run to attempt the [Cleanroom pre-registration discipline](../pathways/prediction_cleanroom.md#open-methodology-problems) the project has been chasing.

**Engine's claim (Stroke 1):** The market's 77% Anthropic confidence is over-priced; a Set-like usurpation by Google or another lab is structurally likely before 2026-06-30.

**What survives the audit:** the *framework-level intuition* that the market may be over-confident on Anthropic's stability — markets often are over-confident on near-term leaders in fast-moving fields. The *specific prediction* (Google specifically usurps) was correctly flagged by Stroke 2 as mythology-grounded, not evidence-grounded.

**Reduced prediction we're willing to commit on record (low confidence):** the LMArena #1 position will be more volatile between now and 2026-06-30 than the market's 77% Anthropic implies. Specifically, we'd expect at least one of the following by 2026-06-30:
- A non-Anthropic lab (Google, OpenAI, or other) holds #1 for at least a 24-hour window in the interval
- The market price on Anthropic drifts meaningfully (e.g., crosses below 65% at some point in the interval)
- The end-of-June resolution is *not* Anthropic

If none of these happens, the Engine's framework-level intuition was wrong on this scenario.

**Confidence:** Low. The Mirror Auditor correctly diagnosed that Stroke 1's evidence base is mythology-heavy, and without Stroke 3's audit-injected re-synthesis (lost to the bug above), we don't have the framework's audited answer — only its raw thesis.

**Validation date:** 2026-06-30. Compare LMArena leaderboard rank + Polymarket resolution price at that date. Update this run record with the actual outcome.

**Falsifies / surfaces issues:**

- **Backend logging discipline broken** — current process not writing to backend.log, so post-hoc diagnostics are impossible without restarting through `run_dev.bat`. Should be fixed independently of the Stroke 3 bug.
- The DispatcherPanel's "Final resolution" rendering condition (only renders if `finalText` differs from last stroke's raw_response) doesn't handle the empty-stroke-3 case gracefully — the user just sees no Final Resolution, no error.

## Second-run verification (2026-05-25 ~03:50 UTC)

After the first run surfaced the empty Stroke 3, two spawned-task fixes shipped:

- **Backend `client.py:277`** — `query_notebook` now retries silent rejection (HTTP 200 + empty answer) up to 3× with 30s exponential backoff (env-tunable via `GANYMEDE_NOTEBOOKLM_QUERY_MAX_ATTEMPTS` / `_QUERY_BACKOFF`). After exhausting attempts: logs WARNING, returns empty string (preserves partial work).
- **Backend `main.py:30-65`** — installs `FileHandler` on the root logger at import, so `backend.log` always gets writes regardless of launch path.
- **Frontend `DispatcherPanel.tsx:490-507`** — renders an amber warning block when the last stroke's `raw_response` is empty/whitespace.

**What this means:** the empty Stroke 3 is NOT a transient flake. Same scenario, same canonical Engine notebook (`0a7d2672-...`), three independent attempts within 90s — all empty. NotebookLM is consistently refusing this Stroke-3 re-synthesis prompt. Root cause is upstream of Agent 2's retry layer.

**Hypotheses for the deterministic refusal (untested):**

1. **Per-session conversation limit / chat-context state** — the Engine notebook's chat history now has Strokes 1+2 baked in; the 3rd rapid-succession query with similar prefix may hit a NotebookLM heuristic.
2. **Audit-injection format** — the orchestrator prepends/appends the Mirror Auditor's output (with framework jargon like *"PATTERN-MATCHING / RIGIDITY ERRORS / DIMENSIONAL GREEDS"*) to the synthesis prompt. The combined prompt could trip a NotebookLM content filter.
3. **Prompt length** — the audit-injected Stroke 3 prompt is meaningfully longer than the bare Stroke 1 prompt. If it exceeds a soft input cap, NotebookLM may silently truncate-to-empty rather than respond.
4. **Persona reset between strokes** — `configure_persona` may need to be re-applied before the Stroke-3 query for the Engine to know how to respond to friction-injected input.

**Diagnostic experiments to try (new spawned task):**

- Re-run with the audit injection stripped from Stroke 3 — does it succeed when the prompt looks like Stroke 1 again?
- Try Stroke 3 on a different scenario — does the failure generalize, or is it specific to the LMArena prompt?
- Capture the exact full Stroke 3 prompt the orchestrator constructs (currently not logged) — visual inspection may reveal the malformation.
- Test whether re-applying `configure_chess_engine` between strokes resolves it.
