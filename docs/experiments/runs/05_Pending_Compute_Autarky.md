## Scenario (Umpire's proposal, verbatim conceptually)

> A collision between **Global AI Compute Cartels** (NVIDIA / Microsoft / OpenAI) and a **National Sovereign Intelligence Declaration** by a major non-aligned economic power (e.g. India or Brazil).

The premise: a state declares it will build domestic AI compute and decouple from the cartel ecosystem. The cartels respond with the full set of tools available to them — pricing, hardware access, regulatory pressure, ecosystem lock-in. The 9D Engine is asked to model the trajectory of that collision across all nine dimensions simultaneously.

## Why the Umpire architected this scenario

When asked to design a "maximum extent" test, the Umpire selected this collision because it forces the Engine to resolve all nine dimensions at once:

- **Economic** — compute costs vs. national GDP, capital flight, tariff response
- **Technological** — proprietary GPU ecosystems vs. open-source / domestic silicon
- **Political** — digital sovereignty vs. global integration, treaty exposure
- **Psychological** — national pride vs. FOMO on AGI capability
- **Temporal** — short-term break-even vs. multi-decade compute trajectory
- **Cultural** — narrative of "tech independence" inside the country
- **Social** — labor market impact, talent flows
- **Environmental** — power grid implications of domestic compute scale-up
- **Ethical** — the moral weight of dependency vs. autonomy

It also stress-tests the **Oracle swarm** harder than prior runs: each subject sits at the bleeding edge of current trade law, semiconductor manufacturing, and AI policy, where the source landscape is thin and contested.

The Umpire's "Strategic Blueprint" should drive the actual hit list — that's a Phase 1 step, not a pre-decision. But based on the scenario contours, plausible Oracle subjects include:
